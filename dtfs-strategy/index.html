Project 05 · Decision brief · Sep 8, 2026

Fold DTFs.co into Shirt.co. Keep DTF as a method, not a consumer brand.

The big transfer companies win as factories: instant checkout, gang sheets sold by the foot, and resolution checks before money changes hands. DTFs.co is attracting hobbyists with cheap ads and SEO, then printing low-dollar, low-quality files. Shirt orders already make more money. Stop competing with Ninja. Route transfer demand into finished apparel and quiet wholesale.

Recommendation: Yes — merge the public DTFs.co site into Shirt.co and phase out consumer transfer e-commerce and paid ads. Do not shut down DTF production. Use DTF to decorate shirts, hats, and contract jobs, and keep the partner transfer portal for print shops that already know how to send print-ready art.

What is going wrong

DTFs.co is built and marketed like a side-hustle transfer shop. Shirt Co is a union print shop whose profitable unit is a finished garment, not a $3 film.

  • The first live DTFs.co order, Jan 23, 2026, totaled $3.59 and came from ads. Meta was billing roughly $800–$900 per cycle around that launch.
  • Chamber Media’s Nov 2025 plan was a heavy Facebook push into mom groups. That audience buys one cheap transfer and uploads a phone photo.
  • Galaxy’s last 30-day report: DTFs.co had 1,821 users and 4 purchases (Sep 2–7). Shirt.co had 4,016 users and 23 quote leads in the same window. August Search Console: Shirt.co 4.11K clicks vs DTFs.co 389 vs Hat.co 168.
  • SEO content is still aimed at hobbyists — “start a baby apparel business with DTF,” eco listicles, gang-sheet explainers. Traffic is up. Order quality is not.
  • Support already sees the art problem. A Sep 7 email to support@dtfs.co was simply “Blurry.” The site still says no minimums, PNG-only, from $2.50 each.
  • Public pricing fights the factory sites and undercuts our own matrix. Homepage 30×24 gang sheet is $12.99 (~$0.018/sq in). The Printavo matrix lists that size at $42.99. Partner portal rates start at $0.03/sq in. The advertised consumer price is below wholesale.
  • Social proof on DTFs.co is Shirt.co hat and embroidery reviews, not transfer customers.

What the big B2B transfer companies do right

Looked at Ninja Transfers, Custom-Transfers.com, DTF Dallas, DTF Center, Sam’s DTF, DTF Transfers Now, and InkMerge. They are not better print shops. They are better transfer stores.

They do Why it works for them Shirt Co position
Instant checkout, not a quote form Hobbyists and shops buy at 11pm. No sales labor on a $14 sheet. Our sales labor is worth more on a shirt quote.
Sell film by the foot / gang sheet AOV moves from one 4×4 to $20–$150+ of packed film. We still promote single transfers from $2.50.
Auto-nest + 300dpi block at checkout Bad files never become paid jobs. Ninja confirms resolution before pay. We print what they send, then hear “blurry.”
Same-day / 24-hour factory rhythm Shops use them as overflow, not as a brand. We already have 2-day and rush. Speed is not the gap.
Specialty SKUs (UV, puff, glitter, foil) Raises AOV without selling a shirt. We already sell shirts, hats, promo, stores. That is the better upsell.
Reprint guarantees, apps, pickup counters Trust for repeat wholesale volume. Copy only for real print-shop accounts, not Facebook moms.

Their published economics are a race to ~$0.014–$0.02 per square inch at volume. Winning that race means industrial RIP, packed printers, and no art help. Shirt Co’s loaded labor assumption in the DTF vs screen study is $28/hour plus $12 overhead. A $12 sheet cannot carry a proof, a reprint, and an ad click.

Should we merge into Shirt.co?

Yes for the public site. No for the equipment.

Merge (consumer DTFs.co)

  • Send dtfs.co to Shirt.co’s DTF service page. Lead with finished shirts. Transfers are an option for shops that already have a press.
  • Kill Meta/Google spend on DTFs.co. Put that money on Shirt.co quotes, union work, stores, and hats — units that can pay for a click.
  • Stop SEO that teaches people to start a transfer side hustle. If Galaxy keeps writing, aim it at print shops and “we’ll print the shirts.”
  • This matches the Feb 25, 2026 website brief: Shirt.co, Hat.co, and DTFs.co were supposed to be pages of one site, with DTFs as /dtf, not a separate storefront.

Keep (DTF as production)

  • In-house DTF for small runs, full-color, names/numbers, and jobs that lose money on screen setup. The production study already points there.
  • The live partner portal at Transfer Orders — size-based jobs, $0.03 / $0.025 / $0.02, free ship at $75, white-label flags. That is the B2B product. Do not advertise it to mom groups.
  • White-label / contract decoration for shops that need union work. That is a Shirt.co sales conversation, not a $12.99 SKU.

Do not try to out-Ninja Ninja

Matching their builder, 24-hour checkout, and $0.02 pricing still leaves us with their customer: low-dollar files and no garment margin. We already make more money when the same art goes on a shirt.

If we kept a transfer store, the floor would have to change

Only worth doing for wholesale accounts. Not a reason to keep the consumer brand.

  • Minimum order around free-shipping ($75) or gang-sheet-only. No $2.50 singles from ads.
  • Block checkout below 300 dpi at final size. Show a Shirt.co upsell: “We’ll print this on shirts instead.”
  • Align public prices with the Printavo matrix. Stop advertising $12.99 for a 30×24 if production is quoted at $42.99.
  • Replace borrowed Shirt.co reviews with transfer-shop proof, or don’t show reviews.

Suggested sequence

  1. Pause DTFs.co paid ads this week. Do not wait on a site rebuild.
  2. Redirect dtfs.co homepage and ads landing pages to Shirt.co DTF + quote form. Offer transfers only after quantity/press questions.
  3. Tell Galaxy to drop hobbyist DTF content and stop treating “purchases” as the DTFs KPI. Shirt.co leads and AOV are the scoreboard.
  4. Keep Transfer Orders for partners. Add dpi validation there if low-quality files are also hitting wholesale.
  5. Park the DTFs.co domain as a 301 once Shirt.co’s DTF page is the canonical URL. Keep the inbox for a while so old customers still reach sales.

What this brief could not see

Stripe was not connected here, so there is no order-level AOV split of DTFs.co vs Shirt.co vs Hat.co. Printavo job export would confirm it. Granola had no meeting that already decided this — only Shirt Co tracking toward about $8M this year. Slack/Missive threads around “DTFS.co” today were not pulled.
